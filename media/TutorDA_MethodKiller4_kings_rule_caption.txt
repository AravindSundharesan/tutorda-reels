∫₀^(π/2) √sin x / (√sin x + √cos x) dx gets ugly with substitution. t = tan x turns it into √t / ((√t + 1)(1 + t²)), and then you're doing partial fractions with √t.

Here's the shortcut, King's rule for definite integrals. With limits 0 to a, swapping x for a − x doesn't change the value. Swap x for π/2 − x: sine becomes cos and cos becomes sine. Add the two versions and the top equals the bottom, so 2I = π/2 and I = π/4.

The rule: ∫₀ᵃ f(x) dx = ∫₀ᵃ f(a − x) dx. When the swap gives you the integrand's partner, add them.
